Open Graph Meta Tags & Social Preview Guide for Websites

Sharing a link on social media or chat apps only works well when the preview card looks clear, accurate, and inviting. When you paste a URL into WhatsApp, X, LinkedIn, or Facebook, the platform fetches metadata from your page to build a preview box with a title, short summary, and image. Using an Open Graph meta tags checker and social preview checker before publishing helps you catch missing fields, awkward text cuts, or stretched images early.

How to Check and Preview Your Social Share Card

Start by entering your page URL, headline, summary, and image link into the editor above. You can switch between the WhatsApp link preview, X card, and LinkedIn or Facebook layout to see how your text wraps on smaller screens. If you are working on a design before uploading assets, you can also pick a local image file to inspect its dimensions right away. Once your fields look balanced, copy the generated HTML tags and paste them inside the <head> section of your page.

Choosing the Right og:image Size and Layout

Visuals do most of the heavy lifting on a social feed. A standard og:image size of 1200 by 630 pixels fits a 1.91:1 ratio that works smoothly across major social cards and messaging apps. Keep important text, logos, and faces away from the outer edges so nothing gets clipped when a platform crops the thumbnail. A crisp image with a reasonable file size also loads faster on mobile connections, which matters a lot for a WhatsApp link preview where readers often check links on the go.

Quick Questions and Answers on OG Tags

Why do some shared links show an old title or missing image?

Social apps and chat tools cache link previews for a while so they do not re-fetch the same page on every single share. If you update your og:title or og:image after publishing, platforms may still display the older version until their cache refreshes or you test with a fresh URL parameter.

Should my OG title match my SEO page title word for word?

Not always. Search engines look at your <title> and meta description for search results, while social readers scan a feed quickly. An og:title can be slightly punchier or clearer for social audiences while still staying honest to what is on the page.

Do I need both Open Graph tags and Twitter card tags?

Adding both is good practice. Many platforms fall back to Open Graph tags when dedicated card tags are absent, while adding twitter:card gives you explicit control over whether X displays a large banner or a compact summary card.

Can I build social preview cards with site builders like Framer or custom HTML?

Yes. Whether you manage page metadata inside a visual builder like Framer or write raw HTML templates for a static site or custom app, the underlying idea is the same: give each page a clear title, helpful description, canonical URL, and high-quality preview image.

OG tags are Open Graph meta tags placed inside the <head> section of an HTML page. They tell social apps and messaging platforms which title, description, page type, and image to show when someone shares a link.
You can check OG tags by viewing the page source in your browser and looking for <meta property="og:..."> tags, or by pasting your details and image into a social preview checker to see how the card looks and whether key tags are missing.
OG stands for Open Graph, a protocol originally introduced by Facebook and now used across social platforms and chat apps. While OG tags do not directly act as a primary search ranking factor, clear social share cards improve click-through rates and brand trust when links are shared.
An OG title is the headline set with <meta property="og:title" content="...">. It controls the main title displayed on social share cards and can be tailored for social readers even if it differs slightly from your HTML <title>.
A widely recommended og:image size is 1200 x 630 pixels with a 1.91:1 aspect ratio, kept under a few megabytes in JPG or PNG format so it loads quickly and avoids awkward cropping on large social cards.
In an Open Graph tag, the content attribute holds the actual value for that property, such as the headline text inside og:title, the summary inside og:description, or the image URL inside og:image.

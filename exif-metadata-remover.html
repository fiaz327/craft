Image Privacy & Metadata Guide

Why You Should Remove EXIF Metadata & GPS Location Before Sharing Photos

When you take a photo with a phone or digital camera, the image file often stores hidden EXIF metadata inside its headers. That data can include exact GPS latitude and longitude, device make and model, software tags, and the date and time the shot was taken. Using a client-side EXIF remover lets you inspect those details and strip them locally before posting photos on forums, marketplaces, social media, or shared links.

location_on

Hidden GPS Location Risks

Photos taken at home, work, or school can embed precise GPS coordinates. Checking and removing GPS data before sharing helps keep your physical location private.

Camera & Device Footprints

EXIF tags frequently record phone/camera models, editing apps, and capture timestamps. Stripping metadata removes those extra file details cleanly.

Quick Questions & Answers

Q1. How do I remove GPS coordinates from a photo?

Pick your file in the box above, see if GPS tags appear in the report, choose your clean export format, and download the updated image. It all happens on your own device with no server upload.

Q2. Can I check EXIF data and remove location before sharing?

Yes. The side panel reads available JPEG EXIF and GPS tags first so you can spot location coordinates or camera info, then exports a stripped copy without them.

Q3. Is EXIF data removed when resizing an image?

Not always. Some image editors or resizing apps retain old EXIF and GPS tags when saving. Re-exporting a clean image or stripping metadata segments removes hidden tags reliably.

Q4. Are my photos uploaded to any server?

No. Every image is inspected and cleaned locally in your browser with zero server uploads.
